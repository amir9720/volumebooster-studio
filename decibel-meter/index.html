How to Measure Sound Levels

1

Start the Meter

Click the "Start Measurement" button. Your browser will prompt you with a security pop-up asking for microphone access.

2

Allow Access

Click "Allow". Without microphone permissions, the web tool cannot physically measure the sound waves in your room.

3

Read the Gauge

The number on the screen will fluctuate in real-time based on the environmental noise. Face your phone's microphone towards the noise source.

4

Check Safety

Look at the text below the numbers. It will warn you if the environment is too loud (e.g., above 85 dB), which can cause hearing damage over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

While highly useful for general reference, a web browser relies on the hardware constraints of your smartphone's microphone. Most mobile microphones are designed for human voice and may max out around 90-100 dB. For professional industrial or legal measurements, a dedicated hardware SPL (Sound Pressure Level) meter is required.
According to the World Health Organization (WHO), prolonged exposure to noise levels above 85 dB (like a busy city street or a loud lawnmower) can lead to permanent hearing loss. Normal conversation is around 60 dB, and a quiet library is about 40 dB.
Absolute zero decibels (0 dB) is the threshold of human hearing, not the complete absence of sound. Even in a very quiet room, there is ambient noise (air conditioning, distant traffic, electrical hum) that registers between 20 to 30 dB.
