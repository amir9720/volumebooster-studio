What is Audio Latency?

Have you ever watched a video on your phone where the person's lips move a split second before you actually hear their voice? Or perhaps you've noticed a frustrating delay when pressing play or pause while using wireless Bluetooth earbuds? That delay is known as audio latency.

Latency is measured in milliseconds (ms) and represents the time it takes for an audio signal to travel from its digital source (like the Mi Music app or browser) to your physical output device (speakers or headphones). While wired headphones usually have near-zero latency (under 10ms), standard Bluetooth headphones can suffer from delays ranging anywhere from 150ms to 300ms due to wireless encoding.

Why Minimizing Latency Matters

Low latency is critical not only for watching movies and videos without lip-sync issues, but also for mobile gaming and real-time audio editing. Using wired connections or modern Bluetooth codecs (like aptX or AAC) significantly reduces lag.
